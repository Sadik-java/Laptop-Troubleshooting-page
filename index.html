Why Is My Laptop Not Charging?

A laptop not charging usually comes with few reasons like loose physical connection, a faulty power adapter, a software glitch or an aging battery

Here are some common causes:

01

Faulty charger/power-adapter

frayed cables, bent pins, or a dead adapter brick often lie at the heart of the issue.

02

Loose or damaged charging port

constant plugging and unplugging can cause the port to become loose or the pins to bend, rendering it unusable.

03

Aging battery

Laptop batteries degrade with age and cycles of charging; after 3-4 years many batteries cannot hold the charge properly.

04

Dust in Port

Sometimes dust get stuck in the port which blocks the connection.

05

Overheating

Some laptops stops charging automatically if internal temperature gets too high

02

How to Fix a Laptop That Is Not Charging?

Before panicking, work through these steps:

01

Check the power outlet

Plug another device into the same outlet to confirm the power supply.

02

Inspect the charger and cable

Look for bent pins, frayed cables or a loose connection at either of the end.

03

Clean the charging port

Power off the laptop and gently clean the dust from the port, use a can of compressed air to blow out any loose dust, and a wooden toothpick to loosen any debris stuck in it.

04

Update or Reinstall Battery Drivers

If it's in device Manager (Windows), Uninstall The Driver in Device Manager, and Reboot, It Should Reinstall Itself.

05

Reset Your Power Management Settings

On Windows, running a power trouble-shooter or resetting the battery driver can fix the issue.

Important: If none of these steps help, the issue is likely hardware-related and may need professional attention.
03

Laptop Plugged in but Not Charging, What Does It Mean?

"Plugged in, not charging" is a particular state that is often displayed on the Windows laptops. Essentially, it means that the laptop is powered on and receiving power from the charger.

However, for some reason, the battery cannot hold a charge and stops growing when the laptop is on. It is not the same as a dead battery since it still has power, not enough to sustain a restart.

The following are the possible reasons why your laptop has stopped charging:

  • The battery is old/degraded and is no longer able to charge beyond a certain point
  • A driver or firmware bug is causing it to report incorrectly what percentage of charge it is at
  • Your charger does not provide the wattage needed for the laptop to charge while in use
  • The battery has been set to only charge to a certain percentage in BIOS or some utility provided by the manufacturer (often seen on business laptops to prolong battery life)
  • A connection issue between the battery and motherboard inside the laptop

How to Check Whether the Problem Is the Charger or Battery

Here are some simple tests through which you can check whether the problem is charger or battery-

01

Swap a charger

Borrow a different charger from your friend or colleague, or test your charger on another laptop with the same brand. If a different charger works then yours is likely faulty.

02

Run a battery report (Windows)

The battery report will show you the health of your battery, its capacity, and the number of charge cycles it has undergone.

powercfg /batteryreport
03

Check the battery health in the system settings (Mac)

In the system settings, you will find information about your battery's condition and performance.

04

Listen and feel for heat

An adapter that is unusually warm or emits a buzzing noise may be failing internally.

05

Only using on AC power

If your laptop has a removable battery, remove the battery when the laptop is turned off, attach the working charger, and turn on the laptop.

When Does a Laptop Need Charging Port Repair?

Some common signs that there’s a problem with your device’s physical charging port (as opposed to an issue with the battery or charger itself) are:

✓ Only charging when the cable is positioned at just the right angle
✓ Wobbliness or unseated feel when plugging in
✓ Physical damage or obstruction of the port
✓ Spotty charging that stops when moved
✓ No illumination of the charging LED (if your device has one) even with a known good charger

Charging ports tend to be damaged on devices that get tossed around in bags or on the go, and are generally not something you can fix yourself due to the cable being soldered to the motherboard.

Laptop Battery Replacement vs. Charging Port Repair

These are two different repairs. The signs, costs, and time required for each are different.

Battery Replacement

The battery on your laptop dies quickly, your laptop says it is plugged in but does not charge, randomly powers off even when it indicates it is at 100% charge, or the battery health is reporting a capacity that is not suitable for your needs.

This is a more simple and straight forward repair than the charging port repair in some laptops depending on the battery type.

The battery replacement will be cheaper than the charging port repair in some cases.

Charging Port Repair

Your laptop is not detecting the charger, the charging port is loose, or the charging port is damaged.

This repair is a bit more complicated since you will need to resolve the charging port on the motherboard.

The charging port repair is a bit more expensive than the battery replacement in some cases.

A professional can tell you which one you need in a few minutes!

When Should You Visit a Laptop Repair Service?

Consider professional repair if:

✓ You have tried another cable and power source, but the problem is still there
✓ The charging port on your device is damaged beyond repair
✓ Your battery's health or capacity is greatly reduced
✓ Your laptop charges inconsistently and only at specific angles
✓ You can smell something burning, or the battery is warm or swollen (in this case, stop using the laptop)
✓ If your laptop is under warranty, you should take it to an official service center to avoid losing your warranty
Safety Warning

Do not try to open the laptop if you do not have sufficient technical knowledge and experience with electronics. A lithium-ion battery can be dangerous if it is damaged.

How to Prevent Laptop Charging Problems

✓ Use the original charger or a certified compatible replacement rather than cheap, uncertified alternatives
✓ Avoid yanking the cable out at an angle; unplug it straight to reduce strain on the port
✓ Keep the charging port clean and free of dust or lint
✓ Avoid leaving the laptop plugged in at 100% for extended periods if your device doesn't have built-in charge management
✓ Don't let the battery repeatedly drain to 0% before recharging
✓ Store and use the laptop in a cool, well-ventilated space to avoid overheating
✓ Periodically check for driver and firmware updates related to power management
✓ Handle the charging cable gently and avoid wrapping it tightly, which can cause internal fraying over time

FAQs About Laptop Charging Problems

1. Why does my laptop say plugged in not charging?

My laptop may say that it is plugged in but not charging because the battery has stopped charging correctly. This happens when the battery needs replacing or because of an issue with the drivers or due to a charge limit set by the BIOS or manufacturer.

2. Is it possible for my laptop to utilise the power from the charger without the battery charging?

Yes, this can happen if your laptop is using power from the charger but the power is not going to the battery. This can be a temporary solution while the battery is being charged but it is not recommended to try and use your laptop this way.

3. Is it ok to continue to use my laptop with this issue?

Yes, this is fine as long as you are not overcharging the battery. However, if you notice that the laptop is getting hot, the battery is puffing out, or there is a smell coming from the laptop then it is best to stop using the laptop straight away.

4. How do I know if it is my laptop or my charger?

The easiest way to test this is to try and see if another charger can charge the laptop. If yes, then you now know that your original charger is faulty.

5. How long does it take to repair a charging port?

It can take anywhere from a day to just over a week, depending on how long it takes to get the correct spare parts, and how long it takes to get into your specific brand and model of laptop to replace it.

Do I need to get my laptop battery replaced?

If your laptop battery pack is easily removed then you may wish to try and replace it yourself, however, this may be quite a task if the laptop battery is built into the laptop. If this is the case then it is best to let a professional replace it and you will avoid the risk of doing any further damage to your laptop while trying to replace the battery.
